The scenario. You work at Contoso Home & Auto, a fictitious department retailer that sells automotive parts, tires, tools, home goods, outdoor equipment, sports gear and seasonal items. Its free loyalty program, Compass Rewards, lets members earn Compass Cash (1 Compass Cash = $1) at stores, online, at partner banners and with a co-branded credit card. A program update on February 1, 2026 lowered the partner fuel earn rate and shortened the inactivity period, and members noticed.
  1. Pick your role in the bar above. Prompts tagged for your role (plus the ones for everyone) stay visible.

Warm-up: Copilot Chat

Run both prompts in Copilot Chat and compare the results. The strong prompt uses the four elements: Goal, Context, Source, Expectations.

0.1

Weak prompt

Everyone 
Write something about the rewards program for my team.

Look for: Generic, vague output. Copilot has to guess the goal, the audience and the format.

0.2

Strong prompt (Goal + Context + Source + Expectations)

Everyone 
Goal: Draft a short weekly huddle note that helps store teams talk about Compass Rewards with customers.
Context: I work at a home and auto retailer. The audience is store associates and service advisors, many of them new or seasonal. Members are frustrated about a recent change to the fuel earn rate.
Source: Use the attached Compass Rewards program guide (section 7, "What changed").
Expectations: 200-250 words, friendly tone, 3 headings, one sample sentence associates can say at the till, and one privacy reminder.

Look for: Notice how each part (Goal, Context, Source, Expectations) changes the result. Attach the program guide first, or paste its text.

Researcher

Deep, multi-step research with citations

Researcher takes more time than Copilot Chat so it can search the web and, at work, the files, emails, meetings and chats you already have access to. It returns a structured report with cited sources. It may ask you clarifying questions first; answer them or say "Go ahead."

Best forMarket scans, briefings, "what do we know about X" questions, pulling together your own work history on a topic.
Not forQuick answers, short rewrites or simple back-and-forth (use Copilot Chat).
TipTell Researcher where to look: the web, your work data, or both. Ask it to separate facts from assumptions.
R1

Loyalty program market scan

Loyalty MarketingPeople Leaders 
Research how large North American retailers have changed their loyalty programs in the last 24 months.
Focus on: tiered vs. paid memberships, coalition or partner programs, app-only personalized offers, and points expiry rules.
Use web sources only and cite each one.
Then compare those trends with the attached Compass Rewards program guide and tell me:
1) three areas where Compass Rewards looks behind the market,
2) two areas where it looks ahead,
3) the risks of copying each trend.
Format: executive summary (5 bullets), a comparison table, then recommendations.

Look for: Citations on every market claim, a clear split between web facts and what comes from the program guide.

Follow-up: Which of your sources are more than 18 months old? Flag them and replace them with newer sources if you can.
R2

Sign-up playbook for the front end

Store Operations 
I manage a [big-box home and auto] store. Research proven ways retail stores increase loyalty program sign-ups at checkout without slowing down the line.
Look for: scripts, staff incentives, signage, app-based sign-up, and how stores measure success.
Give me a one-page playbook with 5 tactics. For each tactic include: what to do, why it works (with a source), effort (low/medium/high), and one metric to track weekly.

Look for: Practical tactics with sources, not generic advice. Effort ratings you can act on.

Follow-up: Turn the playbook into a 10-minute huddle agenda for my team on [Tuesday morning].
R3

Points-dispute best practices

Customer Care 
Research customer service best practices for handling loyalty program complaints, especially missing points, expired balances and earn rate changes.
Then use the attached Compass Rewards program guide (sections 5, 6 and 7) to build an escalation guide for contact centre agents.
Format: a table with columns Situation | What the policy says | What to say to the member | When to escalate.
Cite web sources separately from the program guide.

Look for: Each row ties back to a section of the guide. Empathetic wording you could actually say.

Follow-up: Which situations does the program guide NOT cover? List the gaps I should raise with the loyalty team.
R4

Loyalty liability, explained

Finance 
Research how retailers typically account for loyalty points or in-program currency under IFRS 15 and ASC 606: deferred revenue, breakage estimates and what happens when redemption rates fall.
Explain it in plain language for a non-finance leadership audience.
Include a simple worked example using $1,000,000 of Compass Cash issued and a 70% expected redemption rate.
Note clearly where an organization's own accounting policy or auditors would decide.

Look for: A plain-language explanation, a worked example, and clear caveats. This is learning material, not accounting advice.

Follow-up: What questions should I ask our finance team about breakage if our redemption rate drops from 62% to 49% in one region?
R5

Seasonal category outlook

Merchandising 
Research consumer trends for the next two seasons in these categories: tires, automotive parts, outdoor equipment, sports gear, home goods and seasonal items.
Use web sources from the last 12 months and cite each one.
For each category give: the trend, the evidence, and one way a loyalty offer (for example bonus Compass Cash or tier-only early access) could support it.
Finish with the top 3 categories to prioritize for member offers and why.

Look for: Category-by-category evidence with dates. Loyalty ideas tied to the trend, not generic promos.

Follow-up: Turn the top 3 into a one-slide summary I can share with my category manager.
R6

Catch me up from my own work

Everyone 
Look across my emails, meetings, chats and files from the last [30] days about [project or topic, for example "spring tire season"].
Create a briefing with: what has been decided, what is still open, who owns each open item, upcoming dates, and risks.
List the sources you used for each point. If something is unclear or conflicting, say so instead of guessing.

Look for: This uses YOUR work data, so results depend on what you can access. Check the sources it cites.

Follow-up: Draft a 5-line status update I can send to my manager based on this briefing.

Analyst

Thinks like a data scientist; runs Python on your data

Analyst reasons through data step by step. It can run Python on the files you give it, build charts, and show you the code it ran so you can check its work.

Best forSpreadsheets and CSV files: trends, comparisons, cleaning messy data, charts, simple forecasts.
Not forData you are not allowed to share, or decisions that need an audited source of truth.
TipAttach the CSV first. Ask it to state its assumptions and to show the code. Check one number yourself.
A1

Profile the data first

EveryoneAnalytics & Insights 
Attach compass-rewards-monthly-kpis.csv.
Profile this dataset before analyzing it:
1) describe each column and its data type,
2) list any data quality issues (inconsistent labels, blanks, duplicates) and how you would fix them,
3) after cleaning, give me the 5 most important insights about the loyalty program, each with one supporting number.
Show one chart that tells the most important story.

Look for: It should find 3 rows labelled "west" in lowercase, 2 blank AppActiveMembers values and 1 duplicate row.

Follow-up: Show me the Python code you ran for the cleaning step and explain each line in plain language.
A2

Which region is not redeeming?

FinanceAnalytics & Insights 
Using compass-rewards-monthly-kpis.csv, calculate the redemption rate (CompassCashRedeemed ÷ CompassCashIssued) by region for each month.
Chart the trend by region.
Which region is moving away from the others, and by how much? Compare the first 6 months with the last 6 months.
Then estimate the unredeemed Compass Cash for the last 12 months, in total and for that region.

Look for: North drops from about 62% to about 49% while the other regions stay around 67-73%.

Follow-up: Give me three possible business reasons for this pattern and the data I would need to test each one.
A3

Campaign ROI ranking

Loyalty Marketing 
Attach compass-rewards-campaigns.csv.
For each campaign, calculate ROI as (IncrementalSales − CampaignCost − CompassCashBonusIssued) ÷ (CampaignCost + CompassCashBonusIssued).
Rank the campaigns, chart the result, and compare "App only" campaigns with all other channels.
Recommend 3 campaigns to repeat, 1 to redesign and 1 to stop. Explain each in one sentence a marketing manager would understand.

Look for: Tire campaigns lead. Holiday Mega Bonus 10x and Partner Fuel Bonus barely break even. The win-back campaign loses money.

Follow-up: If we cut the Holiday Mega Bonus multiplier from 10x to 5x, what would ROI look like? State your assumptions.
A4

Voice of the member

Customer CareLoyalty Marketing 
Attach compass-rewards-member-feedback.csv.
1) Calculate Net Promoter Score (promoters 9-10 minus detractors 0-6, as a % of responses).
2) Show the number of comments and average score by Theme and by Channel.
3) Pick the two lowest-scoring themes and quote 2 short comments for each.
4) Suggest one fix for each of those two themes that a store or contact centre could start this month.

Look for: Missing points and expiry confusion score lowest. Remember this is a small comment sample, not the whole membership.

Follow-up: Write the findings as a 5-bullet summary for my team huddle.
A5

Spot the churn spike

Store OperationsPeople Leaders 
Using compass-rewards-monthly-kpis.csv, compare ChurnedMembers by region for February-March 2026 against February-March 2025.
Is any region unusual? Show a chart and put the size of the change in plain language.
Then look at compass-rewards-program-guide.md section 7 (attach it too) and suggest what might explain the timing. Mark which part is evidence and which part is your hypothesis.

Look for: East churn more than doubles in Feb-Mar 2026, right after the February 1 program change.

Follow-up: What should a regional manager in the East ask store managers this week to learn more?
A6

Tier value and a simple forecast

MerchandisingFinanceAnalytics & Insights 
Using compass-rewards-monthly-kpis.csv:
1) For the last 12 months, what share of members and what share of member sales come from each tier (Explorer, Trailblazer, Summit)?
2) Forecast total ActiveMembers for the next 6 months using a simple method. Explain the method and show a chart with the forecast clearly marked.
3) Give me two cautions about trusting this forecast.

Look for: Summit is about 4% of members but about a quarter of member sales.

Follow-up: Turn this into a table I can paste into Excel, with the forecast in its own column.

Prompt Coach

Write, analyze and improve prompts

Prompt Coach helps you structure better prompts. It analyzes a prompt against Goal, Context, Source and Expectations and suggests improvements.

Best forFixing a prompt that gave a weak answer, building reusable prompt templates for your role.
Not forDoing the task itself. Take the improved prompt to Copilot Chat or another agent.
TipIf you don't see it under Agents, select All agents, search for Prompt Coach, then add it.

I am a [your role] at a home and auto retailer with a loyalty program called Compass Rewards.

Look for: A score per element and a much stronger rewrite you can copy.

Follow-up: Now give me a shorter version of the rewritten prompt that still keeps all four elements.

Look for: One question at a time. The final prompt should be specific to your real task.

Follow-up: Turn that prompt into a template with [placeholders] I can reuse every week.

Idea Coach

Brainstorm, refine and plan ideas

Idea Coach helps generate and refine ideas with structured creative techniques, and can plan an agenda for running a brainstorming session.

Best forGetting unstuck, running a team brainstorm, turning a rough idea into an action plan.
Not forFinal decisions. Pressure-test the best ideas with data (Analyst) or research (Researcher).
TipAsk for a specific technique (for example SCAMPER, reverse brainstorming or "How might we") and ask it to rank ideas by impact and effort.
I1

Re-engage members who are not redeeming

Loyalty MarketingStore Operations 
In the North region of our loyalty program, members are earning Compass Cash but redeeming much less than other regions (about 49% vs. about 70%). Stores there are smaller and carry fewer products.
Use "How might we" questions, then generate 15 ideas to help those members get value from their Compass Cash.
Group the ideas into themes and rank the top 5 by impact and effort in a table.

Look for: Ideas specific to small, remote stores (for example online redemption, ship-to-store, partner redemption).

Follow-up: Take the top idea and turn it into a one-page pilot plan: goal, steps, owner, 30-day timeline and success measure.
I2

Plan a 30-minute team brainstorm

Store OperationsPeople Leaders 
Plan a 30-minute brainstorming session for my store team (8 people, mixed roles: cashiers, service advisors, department leads) on this question: "How can we sign up more Compass Rewards members without slowing down checkout?"
Include an agenda with timings, a warm-up activity, one creative technique, how to vote, and how to capture next steps.

Look for: A realistic agenda you could run tomorrow, not a long workshop.

Follow-up: Give me 3 questions to ask if the team goes quiet.
I3

Seasonal bundle offers

MerchandisingLoyalty Marketing 
Use SCAMPER to brainstorm loyalty offers that bundle categories for the winter season. Our categories: tires, automotive parts, tools, home goods, outdoor equipment, sports gear and seasonal items.
Give 2 ideas per SCAMPER letter. Then pick the 3 strongest ideas for Summit-tier members and explain why.

Look for: Cross-category ideas (for example tires + winter emergency kit), with a tier angle.

Follow-up: For the strongest idea, list the risks and the data we would need before launching it.
I4

Reverse brainstorm: redemption

Customer CareStore Operations 
Let's do a reverse brainstorm. List 12 ways we could make redeeming Compass Cash at the till as frustrating as possible for members.
Then flip each one into a positive fix, and mark which fixes a store team or contact centre can start without IT changes.

Look for: The "worst ideas" become a practical checklist. Compare with the redemption complaints in the feedback file.

Follow-up: Turn the no-IT fixes into a one-page checklist for cashiers.
I5

Recognize the team

People Leaders 
I lead a team of [12] in a retail [store / contact centre]. Brainstorm 10 low-cost or no-cost ways to recognize team members who give great loyalty program service.
Include ideas for in-person, virtual and peer-to-peer recognition, and note which ones work for part-time and seasonal staff.

Writing Coach

Feedback on clarity, tone and grammar

Writing Coach gives detailed feedback on clarity, coherence, grammar and tone, helps change the tone of a message, and can translate text.

Best forCustomer emails, announcements, complaint replies, scripts, leadership updates.
Not forMaking up facts. Give it the facts and the policy; it improves how you say them.
TipPaste the text (or attach the file) and say who the audience is. Ask for feedback before a rewrite so you learn something.
W1

Feedback on a rough member email

EveryoneLoyalty MarketingCustomer Care 
Attach or paste member-update-email-DRAFT.md.
Give me feedback on clarity, tone, structure and grammar. List the 5 biggest problems in order of impact, with a short example from the text for each.
Do not rewrite it yet.

Look for: It should catch the long subject line, passive voice, jargon ("stakeholders", "optimizing"), "more then", "18 month" and the buried key facts.

Follow-up: Now rewrite it at about a Grade 8 reading level: 150-180 words, a clear subject line, the 4 changes as bullets, and one clear next step for members.

Customer Care

Career Coach

Skills, goals and career paths

Best forGrowth plans, skill-gap checks, preparing for a career conversation, turning experience into resume bullets.
Not forConfidential HR details. Do not paste performance reviews, salaries or other people's information.

Day-one habits

  1. Start with Goal + Context + Source + Expectations.
  2. Pick the right agent: research → Researcher, data → Analyst, words → Writing Coach, ideas → Idea Coach, prompts → Prompt Coach, growth → Career Coach, learning → Learning Coach.

  4. Check one number or one source yourself before you share.
  5. Never paste customer or employee personal information into a prompt unless your organization has approved it.
